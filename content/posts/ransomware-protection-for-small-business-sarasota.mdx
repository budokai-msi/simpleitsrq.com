## The Short Answer

Ransomware protection for small business is not about buying the most expensive software or living in a state of constant fear. It comes down to five boring, technical, and highly effective controls: immutable backups, multi-factor authentication (MFA) on everything, removing local admin rights, managed endpoint detection, and automated patching. If you have these five things dialed in, you have eliminated about 98 percent of the risk that keeps most business owners in Manatee and Sarasota counties up at night. I have walked into offices on Main Street after an attack, and the difference between a business that recovers in four hours and one that closes its doors forever is almost always one of these five pillars.

## Recommended Gear & Solutions

I do not believe in one-size-fits-all, but over the years, I have found a few specific tools that consistently perform well for the types of businesses we have here in Sarasota and Bradenton.

**Synology NAS with C2 Cloud**
This is for the small office that still needs local file storage but wants protection against encryption. Synology has a feature called Hyper Backup that allows for immutable snapshots. This means even if a hacker gets into your network, they cannot delete your backups because they are locked for a set period. I recommend this for engineering firms and medical practices that handle large files.

**Bitwarden**
I am opinionated about password managers. Most people use the same password for their email, their banking, and their line-of-business software. Bitwarden is an open-source tool that is easy for employees to use. It allows you to share credentials securely within a team without texting passwords or writing them on Post-it notes. It is the first thing I install when I take over a new site.

**ThreatLocker**
This is a bit more advanced, but it is the gold standard for stopping ransomware. It uses a process called allow-listing. Instead of trying to guess which programs are bad, it blocks everything by default and only allows the programs you trust to run. If a ransomware script tries to execute, ThreatLocker simply says no. I suggest this for any business in a regulated industry like finance or healthcare.

## Action Plan for This Week

You do not need to do everything at once, but you should start moving. Here is the sequence I recommend to improve your ransomware protection for small business immediately.

1. **Audit your MFA.** Pick up your phone and try to log into your work email from a private browser window. If it does not ask you for a code or a push notification, you have a problem. Enable MFA for every single user today. No exceptions, not even for the boss.
2. **Check your backup logs.** Do not just assume the backup is running because a green light is on. I want you to try and restore one single file from three days ago. If you cannot do that in ten minutes, your backup system is a liability, not an asset.
3. **Kill the admin rights.** Check your staff computers. If your employees can install software without a prompt for an admin password, your risk of a ransomware infection is ten times higher than it needs to be. Standard users should be standard users.
4. **Review your terminated users.** Look at your active user list in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace. If you see names of people who have not worked for you since the last hurricane season, delete them or disable them immediately.
5. **Schedule a professional scan.** Sometimes you are too close to the problem to see the gaps. Have a local pro run an external vulnerability scan to see what a hacker sees when they look at your office IP address.
