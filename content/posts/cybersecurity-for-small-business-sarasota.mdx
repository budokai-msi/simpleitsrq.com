## The Short Answer

If you want the most protection for the least amount of money, you need to do two things immediately: turn on Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) on every single account you own and start using a dedicated password manager. Cybersecurity for small business Sarasota does not start with expensive hardware; it starts with identity. Most breaches I see in our area happen because someone reused a password they used for their Netflix account on their work email. Once a hacker gets into your email, they have the keys to your entire business. They can reset passwords for your bank, your vendor portals, and your social media. MFA stops that dead in its tracks. Even if they have your password, they cannot get in without that second code on your phone. It is the single most effective thing you can do today.

## Local Impact for Sarasota & Bradenton Businesses

Being a business owner in Sarasota comes with specific tech challenges that people in Silicon Valley do not think about. We have to deal with more than just hackers. We have to deal with the weather and the seasonal nature of our economy. 

First, there is the hurricane factor. I always tell my clients that cybersecurity and disaster recovery are two sides of the same coin. If your only backup is a hard drive sitting on top of your server in an office on Fruitville Road, you do not have a backup. If a storm surge hits or a roof leaks, that data is gone. A local cybersecurity plan must include off-site, encrypted backups that live outside the hurricane zone. I have pulled dead, salt-water-soaked drives out of offices after a bad storm, and believe me, you do not want to be in that position.

Second, we have a huge influx of people during the winter months. Our local networks get crowded. I have seen businesses in St. Armands Circle struggle because their guest Wi-Fi was not separated from their point-of-sale system. When hundreds of tourists are jumping on your Wi-Fi to post photos of their lunch, they are potentially sitting on the same network that processes your credit cards. That is a massive security hole. I often have to go in and segment these networks so that the public traffic stays far away from the business data.

Lastly, the demographic in Sarasota makes us a target. Scammers know there is money here. They target our local professional services with sophisticated phishing attacks. I have seen emails that look exactly like they came from the Sarasota County Clerk of the Court. They are designed to trick you into clicking a link that installs malware. I tell my clients: if an email feels off, even a little bit, call the person who supposedly sent it. A thirty-second phone call can save you thirty thousand dollars in recovery costs.

## Recommended Gear & Solutions

I prefer tools that are easy to manage and do not come with massive monthly fees. Here are the three things I recommend most often to local businesses.

**Bitwarden (Password Management)**
This is for everyone. Bitwarden is an open-source password manager that is very affordable and incredibly secure. I use it myself. It allows you to generate long, random passwords for every site and stores them in an encrypted vault. You only have to remember one master password. It also makes it easy to share passwords securely between employees, which is much better than writing them on sticky notes attached to monitors. I have seen those sticky notes in almost every office from Palmetto to Venice, and they drive me crazy.

**Ubiquiti UniFi (Networking & Firewalls)**
For most small businesses in SRQ, a basic router from Comcast or Frontier is not enough. I like the Ubiquiti UniFi line because it gives you professional-grade features without a subscription. You can set up multiple Wi-Fi networks, create a firewall that actually blocks malicious traffic, and see exactly what is happening on your network from an app on your phone. It is solid hardware that can handle the heat and the demands of a busy office.

**Synology NAS (Local & Cloud Backup)**
I am a big believer in the 3-2-1 backup rule: three copies of your data, on two different media, with one copy off-site. A Synology NAS sits in your office and backs up your computers instantly. Then, it can sync that data to an encrypted cloud provider like Backblaze. This is vital for Sarasota because if our local internet goes down for a week after a storm, you still have your files locally. If the building itself is damaged, your files are safe in the cloud. It covers all the bases.

## Action Plan for This Week

You do not have to do everything at once. Start with these four steps to improve your stance.

1. Audit your logins. Make a list of every major service your business uses: email, banking, payroll, and CRM. Check to see if MFA is turned on for all of them. If it is not, turn it on before you go home today.
2. Install a password manager. Pick a tool like Bitwarden and start moving your passwords into it. Change any password that is short or that you have used on more than one site.
3. Check your backup. Do not just assume it is working. Try to restore a single file from your backup today. If you cannot do it, or if you do not know how, your backup system is broken.
4. Talk to your team. Spend ten minutes at your next staff meeting talking about phishing. Tell your employees that you will never ask them to buy gift cards or wire money via email. Most successful attacks rely on social engineering, not high-tech hacking.
