If you've ever requested three quotes from three [IT companies in Sarasota](/sarasota-it-support) and gotten back numbers so different they might as well be in different currencies, this post is for you. I run an MSP here locally, and I bid against other providers every week. I'm going to tell you what things actually cost, why the spread between quotes is so wide, and which fees to watch for. This is the conversation I have with business owners before they sign anything, written down once so everyone has it.

## The Short Answer

For a typical 10–40 person Sarasota or Bradenton business in 2026, fully managed IT runs **$120–$200 per user per month**, all-in. Below about $100/user you're buying break-fix with a monitoring sticker on it. Above $250/user someone is either stacking compliance work (healthcare, finance) or padding the invoice. One-time projects price separately: an Office 365 migration is roughly $75–$150 per mailbox, a proper network refresh starts around $3,500 for a small office, and a security risk assessment (the kind your cyber insurance carrier keeps asking about) runs $1,500–$4,000 depending on scope.

## Why the Quotes Vary So Much

When I lose a bid to a cheaper provider, it's almost never because we disagree about firewall prices. It's because we're quoting different products wearing the same name.

**Per-user vs per-device pricing.** Per-device sounds cheaper until you count everything: laptops, desktops, phones, tablets, the conference room machine, that network printer. A 15-person office with per-device billing often ends up paying for 45 devices. Per-user covers whatever a human touches. Ask which model the quote uses and make them count.

**Response time SLAs.** "Same-day response" can mean a technician starts looking at your ticket within 8 business hours. "One-hour response" from a local team means someone who can drive to your Bradenton office is assigned within the hour. Those are different labor models with different price tags, and only one of them shows up when your server dies during season.

**Included hours.** Some contracts include unlimited remote support; others include a block of hours and bill $150–$225/hour beyond it. A low monthly number with expensive overage hours is how a "$49/user" plan turns into a $2,400 invoice in March.

## What You Get At Each Price Point

At **under $100/user**: reactive support. Someone answers the phone when something breaks and remotes in. No meaningful monitoring, no patch discipline, no security stack beyond the antivirus that came with the machine. If your business can tolerate a full day of downtime twice a year, maybe this is fine. Most can't.

At **$120–$160/user**: the actual managed services model. Proactive monitoring, patched machines, managed antivirus and DNS filtering, backup verification, vendor coordination when your software company blames your network. This is where most professional offices, law firms, real estate brokerages, dental practices, should sit.

At **$180–$250+/user**: everything above plus a formal security program. Endpoint detection and response, documented [risk assessments](/services), quarterly business reviews, compliance paperwork for HIPAA or cyber insurance, and a named engineer who knows your environment instead of a ticket queue. Regulated industries in Florida generally need to be here, whether they know it or not.

## The Six Fees Hiding In Contracts

I read competitors' contracts when prospects forward them, and these show up again and again:

1. **Onboarding fees**, legitimate (migrations take real work), but they range from $500 to $150 per user with little rhyme or reason. Ask what it includes and whether it's waived with a commitment.
2. **After-hours rates**, fine in principle, but check the definition of "after hours." Some providers define it as anything outside 9-to-5 Monday-Friday, which doesn't match how a real business in tourism-heavy Sarasota operates.
3. **Trip fees**, $75–$150 per on-site visit after the first included visit. For a local provider this should barely exist; it exists to punish you for choosing a cheap remote-only shop.
4. **Hardware markup**, standard is 10–20%. Anything past 25% and you're funding their boat.
5. **Contract auto-renewal with a 90-day notice window**, miss the window and you're locked in another year. Mark the date the day you sign.
6. **"Security surcharge" line items**, EDR, email filtering, and awareness training cost us real money, so passing them through is honest. But they should be itemized, not lumped into a vague fee you can't remove.

## What I'd Tell a Neighbor Over Coffee

Get three quotes, but make them comparable: same user count, same response expectations, ask each one to state their per-user rate, included hours, onboarding cost, and contract term on one page. Then weight local presence heavily. When your file server dies at 7:45 AM during season, the distance between a provider in [Bradenton](/bradenton-it-support) and one in Tampa is measured in lost billable hours, not miles.

And be suspicious of any price that feels too good. The cheapest MSP bid usually means the monitoring dashboard is turned on and nobody is watching it. You'll meet them properly during your first real emergency.

## Action Plan for This Week

1. Pull your current IT spend: monthly invoices plus any project bills from the last 12 months. Divide by headcount. Most owners are surprised by the real number.
2. Request quotes from two additional providers using the one-page comparison format above.
3. Check your current contract for the auto-renewal notice window and calendar it.
4. Ask your provider (in writing) what their average ticket response time was last quarter. Real ones track it.
