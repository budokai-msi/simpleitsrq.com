## The Short Answer
If you can get fiber, get fiber. In Bradenton, that usually means checking for Frontier FiOS availability first. Fiber offers symmetrical speeds, meaning your upload is just as fast as your download. This is mandatory if you use cloud backups, VoIP phones, or video conferencing. If fiber is not available at your specific street address, Spectrum cable is your next best bet. However, you must have a backup. A single connection in Florida is a recipe for a forced day off when a lightning strike hits a transformer down the street. I always recommend a primary fiber line with a secondary cable or 5G wireless failover to keep the doors open.

## Local Impact for Sarasota & Bradenton Businesses
The geography of Manatee County creates some unique challenges for a business internet setup Bradenton. I have seen offices in older parts of town, like near the Village of the Arts or the industrial zones near US-301, struggle with aging copper lines that haven't been updated in decades. Meanwhile, new builds out in Parrish or the east side of Lakewood Ranch are getting fiber piped in before the drywall is even up. You cannot assume that because the office next door has great speeds, you will too. Infrastructure here is very much a block-by-block situation.

During the winter months, we also deal with the seasonal population shift. Our infrastructure in Sarasota and Bradenton gets pushed to the limit when the snowbirds return. I have had clients call me complaining that their internet is slow, only to find out that the shared cable node in their business park is simply over capacity because every seasonal office is back in full swing. Fiber helps mitigate this because you aren't sharing that bandwidth the same way you do with cable. Cable is like a shared highway, while fiber is more like having your own dedicated lane.

Then there is the weather. I have waded through ankle-deep water in flooded offices after a tropical storm to pull servers off the floor and save data. If your internet equipment is sitting on the ground in a storage closet, you are asking for trouble. A proper setup includes mounting your network rack at least four feet off the ground and ensuring your exterior cable penetrations are properly sealed against our horizontal summer rain. I have seen many routers fried because water followed the service cable right into the building and dripped onto the electronics.

## Action Plan for This Week
1. Find your last three internet bills. Look for the contract end date. Many Bradenton businesses are paying 2018 prices for 2018 speeds. You can often double your speed and lower your monthly bill just by asking for a current plan or switching providers.

2. Run a speed test at 10 AM and 3 PM. Use a site like Speedtest.net and write down the results. If there is a massive dip in the afternoon, you are likely on a congested cable node and should look into fiber options immediately.

3. Look at your network closet. If your router is plugged into a cheap power strip instead of a battery backup (UPS), buy a UPS today. Our local power grid flickers constantly during storm season, and those tiny surges kill routers over time. A battery backup will also keep your internet alive during brief power blips.

4. Check for fiber availability. Go to the Frontier and Spectrum websites and plug in your specific address. Coverage changes fast in Manatee County, and what wasn't available six months ago might be at your doorstep today.
